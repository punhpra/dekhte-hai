1. Be flexible where you can

The biggest savings usually come from flexibility:

  • Dates – Flying mid-week (Tuesday or Wednesday) is often cheaper than Friday or Sunday.
  • Airports – Check nearby airports too. A train to another airport can still work out cheaper overall.
  • Trip length – A stay of about 2 days to 4 weeks often has more fare options than a fixed long weekend.

If your dates are locked, that’s fine — you’ll just rely more on timing and alerts.

2. Don’t book the first price you see

Flight prices move. Airlines and sites change fares during the day and overnight. What looks “okay” at lunch might be better the next morning.

A simple habit helps:

  • Note the route and the lowest price you’ve seen.
  • Wait a day or two if you’re not in a rush.
  • Only book when the price is at or below what you’re happy with.

That’s exactly the idea behind our alerts: we remember the lowest fare we’ve seen for your route and only email you when something cheaper appears.

4. A few extra tips that still help

  • Clear cookies / use incognito – Sometimes helps with price testing, though it’s less dramatic than people claim.
  • Check one trusted site and an alert tool – You don’t need ten tabs open. One booking site + one alert is enough.
  • Act when the email arrives – Good fares can disappear. If we ping you and the price still looks right, book soon.
  • Mind the total cost – Cheap base fare + heavy bags + awkward airports can wipe out the saving. Factor that in.

5. Keep it simple

You don’t need to become a full-time deal hunter. Set the routes you care about, go about your week, and let the alert do the boring part.
